# Crew page

A new public page at **/crew** that introduces the people (and one robot) behind CityWide Digital. It is built as a standalone page for review: the current About page stays exactly as it is, and Crew is not linked from the main menu, footer or sitemap until you sign it off.

## What the page will show

**1. Hero**
"Meet the crew" direction copy with the existing glass/inner-page styling used by About and Services (same dark space backdrop, glass panels, mono kickers), so it feels like part of the site.

**2. Featured crew**
- **Alan — Chief Technology Officer.** The only real person today: the founder/CTO who builds the platform and personally owns the technical side of every account.
- **Rob@ ("Rob At") — Agentic Helpbot.** The playful first line of contact for the chat assistant planned for the CWD platform, described as a character who never sleeps, never loses a lead, and answers before a human can. Clearly presented as *in development* rather than a live chatbot, so nothing pretends to work yet.

**3. Niche crew** — one card each for Restaurants, Service companies, Wholesale, Retail and eCommerce. Each card: portrait, first name, title, niche, a short blurb about why they specialise in that niche, then the two closing lines in the requested format:
- Favourite movie: (the film the suit is from)
- Favourite saying: (a memorable line from it)

Placeholder first names (easy to swap for the real partners): **Nadia** (Restaurants), **Owen** (Service companies), **Priya** (Wholesale), **Cole** (Retail), **Sam** (eCommerce).

A short, clearly worded note explains that the niche crew represent independent white-label partner agencies working within the CityWide platform — so prospects understand they are specialists, not employees.

**4. Closing CTA**
Standard end-of-page call to action pointing to Contact and the demo booking, matching the rest of the site.

## Portraits

Seven original portraits, generated for this page in a single art direction: **painted, semi-realistic caricature** — slightly exaggerated features, real human proportions, film-poster lighting, dark backdrop. Not cartoon, not 3D, not photographic. Torso-up, helmet off and held at the side, each in a visibly different suit and palette so the grid reads as seven distinct characters.

The suits evoke each film rather than reproducing licensed costume designs (and Rob@ is a Bender-style retro bending robot rather than an exact replica). The film names still appear in the text, which is fine as reference.

## What gets built

- [ ] A Crew page loads at its direct link with hero, featured crew (Alan, Rob@), the five niche cards and a closing CTA.
- [ ] Every crew card shows a distinct painted space-suit portrait, first name, title, niche blurb, favourite movie and favourite saying; if a portrait fails to load, the card still reads cleanly.
- [ ] Rob@ is presented as the planned chat helper, with an honest "in development" status instead of a working-chat claim.
- [ ] A visible note explains the niche crew represent white-label partner agencies.
- [ ] The page works on phone and desktop widths without horizontal scrolling.
- [ ] The menu, footer, sitemap and About page remain unchanged, and no link to Crew is added anywhere.

## Constraints

- About page, navigation, footer, sitemap and every other page stay unchanged.
- No new menu entry; during review the page is reachable by its direct link only.
- Responsive from phone to desktop; each portrait card degrades cleanly if an image is slow or missing.
- Copy avoids inventing credentials, client counts or team size — the niche crew are described as partner specialists, and Rob@ as planned.

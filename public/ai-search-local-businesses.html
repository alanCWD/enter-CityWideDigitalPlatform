AI tools do not maintain a separate directory you can claim. When someone asks ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity or Google's AI Mode for a local recommendation, the answer is assembled from sources those systems already trust: indexed web pages, Google Business Profile data and consistent listings across the web. CityWide's job is to keep those sources saying the same, accurate thing.

What AI search is

AI search tools answer questions directly. A customer might ask, "Which Victoria café is open late on Sunday?" The tool assembles a response from sources it can read, then names the businesses it is most confident about.

01 / Answers

AI answers

Summaries that answer a question directly and link to the sources they used.

02 / Recommendations

Assistant recommendations

A customer asks for the best option nearby and receives a short list of businesses.

03 / Agents

Agents

Tools that compare options, check availability or act on a customer's behalf.

Google's own guidance is unusually clear: to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, a page must be indexed and eligible to show a snippet. There are no additional technical requirements. Google's AI features guide treats AI visibility as a continuation of normal search, not a separate channel.

How AI picks local businesses

Google's AI features are built on its Search systems, and local ranking still comes down to relevance, distance and popularity: how well the profile matches the query, how close the business is, and how well known and reviewed it is.

Consistency matters for the same reason it matters in search. When hours, address and services match across sources, there is less for a tool to get wrong. When they conflict, the safe answer is often to skip the business.

AI tools are not omniscient. They quote what they can read, and they can only be as current as the sources they find.

Where businesses lose visibility

  • Google shows one set of hours and the website shows another.
  • Directory listings are years out of date.
  • The website blocks crawlers or hides its content behind scripts.
  • The Google profile is thin, with few reviews and no recent posts.
  • Nobody owns the upkeep.

AI tools read data literally. Vague copy, missing hours or a website that cannot be crawled give them little to work with — and plenty of reasons to choose a competitor instead.

On llms.txt and AI-specific files

You may hear that an llms.txt file is the key to AI visibility. It is optional, Google Search ignores it, and no AI platform has confirmed it improves recommendations. CityWide publishes one on its own website as a summary for tools that choose to read it — not as a ranking lever. Accurate data and a crawlable site matter more.

Frequently asked questions

Can you guarantee I'll show up in ChatGPT?

No one can. CityWide improves the accuracy and consistency of the information AI tools find. Whether a tool recommends a business is up to that tool.

Do I need an llms.txt file?

It is optional, and its value for AI visibility is unproven. Google Search ignores it, and no AI platform has confirmed it helps. Accurate data and a crawlable site matter more.

Will this replace my SEO?

No. It builds on the same foundation: a strong Business Profile, consistent listings and a fast, readable website.

Does CityWide put my business inside ChatGPT?

No. There is no paid placement inside these assistants. CityWide makes the public information about your business accurate and consistent, so a tool that looks has a better chance of getting the details right.

How do I test my visibility?

Ask an AI assistant the questions your customers would ask. Check whether it recommends you and whether it gets your hours, services and location right.

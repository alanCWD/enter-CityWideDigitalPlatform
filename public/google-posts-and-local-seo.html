Do Google posts and social media actually help your SEO?

Google posting, social syndication and website content all contribute to local search visibility — through three different mechanisms. The benefit compounds when all three run together.

Guide · Reviewed 25 November 2026

Yes. Google posts, social syndication and website content all contribute to local search visibility — but through three different mechanisms, not one. We separate them because the distinction changes what a business should expect. Google does not treat post frequency as a direct local ranking factor, and likes and shares are not Google ranking signals. What does work is the combination: a current Google profile, consistent distribution and useful website pages built around the services a business sells.

Three mechanisms, not one

One update can do three separate jobs at once. Each contributes something different, and none replaces the others.

01 / Google

Google posting

Strengthens the business's presence inside Search and Maps with current, customer-facing updates.

02 / Social

Social syndication

Expands where customers can encounter the business, and creates more paths back to the website.

03 / Website

Website content

Creates pages Google can crawl, index and rank for specific services and local searches.

Posting to Google strengthens the business's search presence

A post published from CityWide to Google Business Profile lets a business:

  • Showcase specific services and products.
  • Publish relevant local information.
  • Promote seasonal offers and events.
  • Give prospective customers more reasons to contact the business.
  • Keep the Google profile useful and current.

Google does not officially identify post frequency as a direct local ranking factor. Its published local ranking guidance centres on relevance, distance and popularity. A post earns its place by giving customers current, useful information, and by giving Google more detail about what the business offers.

Social syndication expands distribution, not authority

This is where publishing from one place pays off. Through CityWide, a single post reaches Google Business Profile, Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn and the other connected platforms — more opportunities for customers to encounter the business.

It also generates:

  • Referral traffic to the website.
  • Brand awareness.
  • Customer engagement.
  • Shares and mentions.
  • Opportunities for other websites to discover and reference the business.

The same qualification applies: likes and shares are not established direct Google ranking factors. Social posts can be discovered in Google Search, but syndication does not create SEO authority by itself.

The website is where the real SEO opportunity sits

A plumbing company in Victoria, BC is a useful example. Its updates cover:

  • Hot water tank installations.
  • Emergency plumbing repairs.
  • Drain cleaning.
  • Bathroom renovations.
  • Seasonal plumbing maintenance.

Through CityWide, those updates are distributed across Google and social while the website holds synchronised business information. But the website is doing different work: it gives Google pages it can crawl and index.

Here is the distinction that matters. Synchronising Google Business Profile information is not the same as creating SEO landing pages. If that plumber wants to rank for searches such as:

  • Emergency plumber Victoria.
  • Drain cleaning Victoria.
  • Hot water tank replacement Victoria.

… the website needs useful, relevant content addressing those services and searches. A Google post about drain cleaning does not create a dedicated drain-cleaning landing page. Google's SEO Starter Guide explains how useful website content and links help search engines discover and understand pages.

The weekly routine we recommend

For a typical local business, three posts a week keep the profile current without requiring a content team:

Monday

A service spotlight.

Wednesday

A recent project or customer success story.

Friday

An offer, a seasonal tip or a frequently asked question.

Publish to Google and distribute appropriate versions to social. Then measure:

  • Google Maps visibility.
  • Organic website impressions.
  • Website clicks.
  • Phone calls.
  • Enquiries.
  • New customers.

Review after 8–12 weeks. Local visibility moves slowly, and a two-week sample misleads more often than it informs.

Why we don't promise ranking improvements

We do not promise automatic ranking improvements, because no provider honestly can. What we do promise is a consistent foundation — accurate business information, a current profile, and a website and channels that stay aligned — so that quality content and a strong reputation have something reliable to build on.

That is the realistic version of SEO for a local business: not one post that changes everything, but a presence that stays current, stays accurate and keeps giving customers reasons to choose the business.
